Abstract (summary; see the JSE page for the original):
  A table of the population at risk and the deaths in "an unusual episode" had long been used in
  classrooms as an exercise in deducing the context of data. The paper identifies the episode
  (the sinking of the Titanic), examines discrepancies between the table and the "solution" that
  had circulated, and gives a fully documented version of the data for classroom use. The revised
  table, which includes the crew, is based on the British Board of Trade inquiry report:
  2,201 people and 1,490 deaths.

Use in this repository:
  Source of site/CART/data/titanic.csv: datasets::Titanic (a 4-way table of 2,201 people)
  expanded to one row per person, with Survived replaced by Died (1 = did not survive).
  Script: data-raw/CART_example_data.R
  Cross-tabulating the CSV reproduces datasets::Titanic exactly. According to R's ?Titanic,
  Dawson (1995) also provides the data as one record per person.
